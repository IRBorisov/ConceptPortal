Business app · 06 / 10 · Planning · ЯРЭ / Portal

SLA commitment & breach checker

Model service commitments, incident clocks, and breach predicates so support ops can explain why an SLA fired — using ЯРЭ over tickets and contracts.

Business purpose

Replace opaque SLA scripts with transparent definitions tied to contract tiers and calendar rules.

Actors: Customer success, support ops, legal, SRE.

Why ЯРЭ + Concept Portal

  • Breach is a predicate over ticket events and commitment relations.
  • Tiered response times fit S# + F# lookup.
  • Portal schemas align legal contract language with ops interpretation.

Concept Portal usage

  • КС Biz/SLACore; contract tiers as library schema reused across accounts.
  • Per-customer КМ or filtered model values; keep raw ticket dumps out of schema.
  • Agents recompute D1 for reports via evaluate/recalculateModel.

rstool-helper delivery workflow

  1. Agree timestamp interpretation in conventions (epoch minutes).
  2. Build F1/P1; validate with known breach fixtures.
  3. Portal publish for CS managers.
  4. Optional: synthesize calendar/business-hours schema later.

Tooling loop

ensureSession → applySchemaPatch (core X/C/S)
analyzeExpression → patch D/F/P/A/T
setModelValues → evaluate / recalculateModel
listDiagnostics → fix → commitStep
exportPortal({ kind: 'schema' | 'model' }) → Portal library

MVP roadmap

  1. Simple wall-clock response SLA
  2. 24×7 vs business-hours flag
  3. Breach report D1
  4. Multi-severity commitments

Risks & reductions

  • Full calendar algebra may exceed comfortable ЯРЭ — keep complex schedules external, feed boolean inputs.
  • Mixing severity labels as free text — use X3 elements with conventions.

Success criteria

Schema ready

No expression/schema diagnostics; conventions in business language; review checklist passed.

Operations adopted

At least one consumer evaluates the key predicate/statement on real sample data; Portal version pinned in the runbook.
